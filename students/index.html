For students

Take part in Lovelace

Free to attend, with travel and accommodation covered for everyone whose poster abstract is accepted. Come and share your work, hear talks from women in academia and industry, and meet students from across the UK.

Who can take part

Lovelace is open to women and non-binary students studying taught university courses in the UK — that covers foundation year, undergraduate, year-in-industry, and taught postgraduate (MSc) students in Computing and related subjects. The event itself is free and open to all to attend, including men, but the poster contest and travel support are specifically for women and non-binary students.

The poster contest

The centrepiece of the day is a poster contest open to any woman or non-binary student on a taught computing course. You submit a short written abstract (around 250 words) describing your own work — a project, dissertation, piece of research, or something you've built — and if it's accepted you present it as a poster on the day.

Posters are judged by year group, so first-years aren't competing against final-year or MSc students. Cash prizes are awarded in each category, thanks to sponsorship — historically up to £300 for first place and £200 for runner-up in a category, plus additional prizes such as a People's Choice award voted on by attendees.

Why it's worth it

Beyond the prize money, students consistently say the real value is the people: meeting women and non-binary students from other universities, talking to speakers and sponsor representatives about careers in computing, and getting both formal (talks, panels) and informal (conversations over lunch) advice on working in the industry. Sponsor companies also bring recruiters, so it's a genuinely useful stop if you're job- or placement-hunting.
